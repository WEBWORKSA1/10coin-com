Identify

Check your coin in 5 steps

1 · Country & denomination

Read the legend. “E PLURIBUS UNUM” = USA; “ELIZABETH II D·G·REGINA” with a maple leaf = Canada.

2 · Date & mint mark

US mint marks (P, D, S, CC, O) sit near the date or on the reverse. No mark often means Philadelphia.

3 · Metal

Look at the edge: a solid silver edge (no copper stripe) on a pre-1965 US coin means 90% silver.

4 · Errors & varieties

Doubled letters, missing mint marks, off-centre strikes, extra details — use a 5–10× loupe.

5 · Condition

Wear on high points determines grade. Try our 60-second grading quiz.

Coin value FAQ

Is my 1943 steel penny valuable?

Usually not — over a billion were made, and circulated ones sell for cents. The valuable 1943 cent is the rare bronze version, which is not attracted to a magnet.

Are wheat pennies worth anything?

Most common-date wheat cents (1909–1958) are worth 3–10 cents each. Key dates such as 1909-S VDB, 1914-D, 1922 No D and 1931-S, and varieties like the 1955 doubled die, are worth far more.

How do I know if a coin is silver?

US dimes, quarters and halves dated 1964 or earlier are 90% silver; Canadian 10¢ to $1 coins dated 1966 or earlier are 80% silver (plus some 1967–68 issues). Silver coins have no copper stripe on the edge.

Should I get my coin graded?

Grading by PCGS or NGC usually makes sense when a coin could be worth several hundred dollars or more, or when authenticity matters (e.g. key dates and famous errors). Ask us first — it’s free.
