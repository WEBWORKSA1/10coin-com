Collectors · 4 min

Coin collecting: the 10 rules

  1. Never clean coins.
  2. Hold by the edges.
  3. Buy the book before the coin.
  4. Learn the key dates in your series.
  5. Buy certified coins for anything expensive.
  6. Store in inert holders (no PVC).
  7. Keep a simple inventory with photos.
  8. Compare at least 3 dealer quotes.
  9. Condition beats quantity.
  10. Collect what you enjoy.
Bullion · 3 min

Gold & silver basics

Bullion is priced on metal content. A troy ounce is 31.1 g (heavier than a kitchen ounce). You pay spot + premium when buying and usually receive spot − spread when selling. Sovereign coins (Eagles, Maples, Krugerrands) are the easiest to resell. The gold/silver ratio shows how many ounces of silver buy one ounce of gold.

Crypto · 5 min

Crypto for absolute beginners

A cryptocurrency is a digital asset recorded on a public ledger (blockchain). Market cap ranks size; volatility is high — double-digit daily moves happen. Use reputable, regulated exchanges, turn on 2-factor authentication, consider a hardware wallet for long-term holdings, and never share a seed phrase. Only invest what you can afford to lose.

Collectors · 3 min

Errors vs. varieties

Errors are one-off mistakes in striking (off-centre, broadstrike, wrong planchet). Varieties come from a die and repeat on every coin it struck (doubled dies, repunched mint marks). Both can multiply value — but post-mint damage cannot.

Sellers · 4 min

How to sell coins for the best price

Separate bullion from numismatic pieces. Get melt value for silver/gold. Certify high-value coins first. Compare local dealers, online buyers and auctions. Understand fees (auctions often charge the seller 10–20%). Never ship uninsured.

Tax · 3 min

Records that save you money

Keep dates, costs and receipts for every purchase — coins, bullion and crypto. Many countries tax gains on collectibles and digital assets. Good records make reporting simple. Rules vary by country; talk to a qualified professional.

Grading

The Sheldon 1–70 scale, simplified

GradeNameWhat you see
1–3Poor / Fair / About GoodBarely identifiable; date may be worn away.
4–10Good / Very GoodHeavily worn; main design and outline visible.
12–15FineModerate even wear; all lettering clear.
20–35Very FineLight-to-moderate wear; major details sharp.
40–45Extremely FineLight wear on high points only.
50–58About UncirculatedTraces of wear; most luster remains.
60–70Mint StateNo wear. Numbers rise with fewer marks and better luster.

60-second grading quiz

1. Look at the highest points (cheek, hair, feathers).

2. Is there mint luster (cartwheel shine) when you tilt it?

3. Can you read every letter and the full date?

Stay safe

10 red flags of coin & crypto scams

  • “Guaranteed” returns or doubling your money.
  • Pressure to act “today only”.
  • Requests for your wallet seed phrase or password.
  • Payment by gift card, wire only, or crypto to a stranger.
  • “Rare” coins sold far below known value.
  • Uncertified “ultra-rare” errors on marketplaces.
  • Fake grading holders (check the certificate number online).
  • Unsolicited DMs offering investment help.
  • Look-alike websites and apps.
  • Celebrity “endorsements” you can’t verify.
Glossary

Coin & crypto glossary

All-time high (ATH)
The highest price an asset has ever traded at.
Altcoin
Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin.
Assay
Testing a metal to determine its purity.
Blockchain
A shared, append-only ledger that records transactions in linked blocks.
Brilliant Uncirculated (BU)
A coin with no wear that retains its original mint luster.
Bullion
Precious metal valued primarily by weight and purity.
Cameo
Frosted design elements contrasting with mirror-like fields, common on proofs.
Certified coin
A coin authenticated, graded and sealed by a third-party service such as PCGS or NGC.
Cold wallet
Crypto storage kept offline, such as a hardware wallet.
Die
The engraved steel tool that strikes the design into a coin.
Doubled die
A die variety where design elements appear doubled on every coin it strikes.
DCA (dollar-cost averaging)
Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price.
Exchange
A platform for buying, selling and trading crypto assets.
Face value
The legal-tender value stamped on a coin.
Field
The flat background area of a coin’s design.
Fineness
Metal purity expressed in parts per thousand (e.g. .999).
Gas fee
The network fee paid to process a blockchain transaction.
Junk silver
Circulated 90% or 80% silver coins traded for metal value.
Key date
The scarcest, most valuable date in a series.
Legend
The main lettering on a coin.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price.
Luster
The reflective shine produced by the minting process.
Market cap
Price multiplied by circulating supply.
Melt value
The value of a coin’s metal content at current spot prices.
Mint mark
A letter showing which mint produced the coin.
Mintage
The number of coins struck of a given issue.
Mule
A coin struck with two dies not meant to be paired.
Numismatics
The study and collecting of coins, tokens and paper money.
Obverse
The front (“heads”) side of a coin.
Planchet
The blank metal disc that is struck into a coin.
Premium
The amount paid above melt or spot value.
Private key / seed phrase
Secret credentials that control a crypto wallet. Never share them.
Proof
A specially struck coin with mirror fields, made for collectors.
Reverse
The back (“tails”) side of a coin.
Slab
The sealed plastic holder of a certified coin.
Spot price
The current market price for immediate delivery of a metal.
Stablecoin
A crypto asset designed to track a fixed value, usually one US dollar.
Troy ounce
31.1035 grams — the standard unit for precious metals.
Variety
A repeatable difference from the normal design caused by the die.
Volatility
How much and how quickly a price moves.
